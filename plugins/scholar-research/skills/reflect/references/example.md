# Worked example: reflect

Neutral topic, synthetic setting. The project is a fictional paper project; the records named here are invented
for the example (see `examples/research` for a runnable version).

## The talk

**Scholar:** I keep noticing that flat icons in mobile apps took off around the same time as a minimalist look in
print editorial design. I am not sure it means anything though. Maybe it was just that everyone got the same new
design tools around then. Or maybe the apps copied magazines. Hmm, or magazines copied apps. I don't know.

## The reply

**Mirror.** You have a hunch that flat icons in apps and minimalism in print editorial design rose together, but
you are unsure whether that is influence (in either direction) or a shared change in tools.

**Record check.** Nothing recorded on flat icons yet. Related: INS-0002 (shared tooling may explain overlaps,
accepted as a working hypothesis, evidence leads only, two sources not read in full) and the open question under
RQ1 about which field moved first. Known gap: no verified support yet for any direction of influence.

**Rival explanations.**
1. Shared tooling: both fields adopted the same software or fonts at about the same time. Cheapest to test: date
   the tooling releases and compare with the artefact dates.
2. Selection: the artefacts that survive in archives are the minimalist ones, so the overlap is partly a sampling
   effect.

**Next investigation (your call).** (a) A secondary-source search on "flat design" with "editorial design" and on
design tooling history; I would name the databases and strings and tell you what the search did not cover.
(b) A coding table of the artefacts you already have, by period and domain. I would not draw an arrow between the
two fields yet; any chart would show counts only.

**Capture.** I have this on the session list as a hypothesis, not a finding. I will show it at the next pause.

## What was not done

No paragraph was drafted. No source was cited. The RQ was not changed. The hunch was not linked to INS-0002 as
support; it is only marked as related.

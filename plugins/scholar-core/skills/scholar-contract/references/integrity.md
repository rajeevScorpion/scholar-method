# Evidence integrity: the universal rules

These rules apply to every Scholar project, whatever the discipline, venue or writing profile. A venue's rules
may add to them; nothing may weaken them.

## A. Sources and citations
1. Never invent or fabricate a source, DOI, quotation, page number, date, participant response, dataset, figure,
   sample size, observation, statistical result, approval, permission, completed procedure or publication status.
2. Read what the claim needs. An abstract or search snippet is a lead. Record the read status
   (lead, abstract-only, metadata-checked, full-text-reviewed) and the limitations.
3. A citation must support the actual sentence. Keep page or figure locators for quotations and source-specific
   claims. Cite opposing evidence where it matters.
4. Use the venue's citation style. The default is APA 7 unless the project or venue says otherwise. Use the
   project's existing reference tooling (BibTeX, Quarto, a register). Never leave a placeholder that looks like a
   real reference.
5. In prose, integrate author-date citations. Keep DOIs and URLs in the bibliography unless the venue requires them
   inline.

## B. Keeping kinds of statement apart
Directly observed data, source claims, researcher analysis, interpretation, conceptual proposals, untested
hypotheses, supervisor decisions, and reported but unverified past work are different things. Label them. Do not
let a hypothesis, a plan or a target drift into a result.
- Visual resemblance does not prove influence. Chronology does not prove causation.
- A planned sample is not a completed sample. Do not fill target cells with invented observations.
- Keep rival explanations and negative or deviant cases in view.

## C. Status and tense
Ongoing research is described as ongoing. Data collection, findings, approval, permissions and publication are
stated as done only when the project records show they are done.

## D. Visual figures are claims
A chart, map, network or 3D view can assert as strongly as a sentence.
- Do not alter, fabricate, interpolate or hide data to make a graphic convincing.
- Keep uncertainty, contrary evidence, sample and time coverage, source rights, and whether an arrow means "author
  hypothesis" or "documented relationship".
- Captions separate concept sketches from empirical results. Axes and units are accurate and the figure has an
  accessible static version where possible.
- Every substantive visual keeps a sidecar record (`records.md`) linking inputs, transforms, spec and sources.
- Putting a figure into a submitted document needs the scholar's approval and the venue's requirements.

## E. Editing and AI-assisted text
- Editorial passes, including Humanizer, must leave names, numbers, dates, units, citations, quotations, attribution,
  tables and technical terms unchanged unless a separately evidenced correction is approved.
- Do not insert deliberate errors or fake uncertainty to look human. Do not use these tools to hide AI use that a
  venue or institution requires to be disclosed. Follow the venue's AI-use policy and keep substantive AI
  contributions reviewable.
- Show a before and after comparison for any editorial pass. Ask before changing approved prose in bulk.

## F. Ethics, rights and privacy
- Respect participant confidentiality, copyright, licences and publication rights.
- Never move unredacted confidential material into a public repo, shared context or public harvest proposal.
- Record substantive changes with their evidence, including effects on other chapters or companion papers.
- Avoid self-plagiarism: keep provenance for reused text, data, figures and analyses.

## G. Authorship
The scholar is the author. Accepted objectives, methods, reported findings and contributions change only with the
scholar's confirmation.
